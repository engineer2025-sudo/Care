# CareSphere — final 10-slide narration

Target: 2:56. App footage replaces the slideshow between slides 6 and 7.

## 1 · 0:00–0:10 · Title and purpose

I'm Arnav Kochar. CareSphere AI combines routines, self-paced activities, human connection and visit preparation for older adults, autistic people and their families.

## 2 · 0:10–0:20 · The design problem

Daily support should not feel like another task. CareSphere brings reminders, low-pressure activities and ways to connect into one predictable place, with the person in control.

## 3 · 0:20–0:32 · What the app does

There are four areas: activities and sensory tools; mood, routines and medication self-check-ins; Coffee Circles for video conversations; and a visit brief made from information you choose.

## 4 · 0:32–0:46 · Four self-paced games

Feelings Explorer has no correct answer. Pattern Recall lets you repeat a sequence. Picture Pairs reveals matching cards, and Picture Story explores a sequence at your pace. All are untimed and unscored.

## 5 · 0:46–0:58 · Sensory choice and research

Breathing and optional soundscapes are user-controlled. Accessibility research informed predictable navigation and sensory choices. Game research is promising but varied; these activities are not validated therapy.

## 6 · 0:58–1:01 · Switch to the running app

Let’s see CareSphere in action.

## APP · 1:01–2:21 · Real app demonstration

Here I watch a pattern and repeat it. I can replay or stop. In Picture Pairs, I reveal and compare cards without a countdown. I choose when rain starts and stops. Back on Overview, I record a mood and finish a routine. Coffee Circles provides real Jitsi room links. Before a visit, I select fields for a brief and inspect the exact text. It's user-prepared information, not a verified medical record.

## 7 · 2:21–2:33 · Tools, languages and privacy

The web app uses JavaScript, React and Web Audio. The native iPhone app uses Swift and SwiftUI. Care data is encrypted locally; Jitsi calls and online references use external services.

## 8 · 2:33–2:45 · A specific coding challenge

Old Bluetooth callbacks could update a new session. A generation counter rejects stale callbacks. Each accepted sample stays labeled live or simulated, including when exported.

## 9 · 2:45–2:52 · Next step and learning

Next: co-design and usability testing. A working feature isn't proof of real-world benefit.
