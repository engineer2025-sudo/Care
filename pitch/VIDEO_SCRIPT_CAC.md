## 1 · 0:00–0:12 · Name, purpose and audience
<!-- NARRATION -->
I'm Arnav Kochar. CareSphere AI helps older adults, autistic people and their families manage everyday routines, enjoy self-paced activities, connect with others and prepare for care visits.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 2 · 0:12–0:25 · What does it actually do?
<!-- NARRATION -->
There are four parts: activities and sensory tools; mood, routines and medication reminders; Coffee Circles for video conversations; and a visit brief built from information you choose. Here is what using them looks like.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 3 · 0:25–0:42 · Games, not just a tab name
<!-- NARRATION -->
Activities are a core feature. Feelings Explorer lets you choose a feeling without a right answer. Pattern Recall lets you repeat a sequence. Picture Pairs reveals matching cards, and Picture Story explores a sequence at your pace. These are untimed, unscored activities—not cognitive tests.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 4 · 0:42–0:54 · Sensory tools
<!-- NARRATION -->
Breathing follows a four-four-six rhythm. You can start, pause or leave. Four optional soundscapes run through Web Audio. Text size, contrast, palette and motion settings let people choose a comfortable interface.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 5 · 0:54–1:08 · Research and limits
<!-- NARRATION -->
AASPIRE accessibility research informed predictable navigation and sensory choices. Game-based research suggests potential in some settings, but studies vary. It does not prove CareSphere works as therapy. These activities are optional practice and enjoyment; co-design and usability evaluation come next.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 6 · 1:08–1:20 · Other everyday features
<!-- NARRATION -->
Routines and medication self-check-ins support the day. Coffee Circles use real Jitsi rooms. Before a visit, users choose what to include in a brief and inspect the exact text. A medication check-in is a self-report, not a verified dose.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 7 · 1:20–2:00 · Replace this slide with real screen recording
<!-- NARRATION -->
Here I start Pattern Recall, watch the pads, and repeat the sequence. I can replay or stop. In Picture Pairs, I reveal cards without racing a clock. Next I choose rain, then stop it. Breathing also pauses when I choose. Back on Overview, I record a mood and complete a routine. Finally, I select fields for a visit brief and preview the text before downloading it.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

### Recording actions for those 40 seconds
- 0–12s: Activities → Start pattern → repeat the displayed pads → stop.
- 12–18s: Picture Pairs → reveal two cards. Do not fake a match.
- 18–23s: Rain → Stop. Keep volume comfortable.
- 23–27s: Breathing → Begin session → Pause.
- 27–32s: Overview → choose a mood → tick a routine.
- 32–40s: Care Circle → Prepare visit brief → select fields → exact preview.

Use actual screen footage, not a slide-only video. Rehearse and cut between genuine actions if scrolling takes too long. Seed fictional data, label it as sample data, and never show private patient data. Do not place an SOS call or invent a live sensor connection.

## 8 · 2:00–2:11 · Privacy, accurately
<!-- NARRATION -->
Care data is encrypted locally. Sharing is a deliberate action. Jitsi calls and online references use external services; they are not offline. On iPhone, an optional downloaded model can generate Health Guide answers locally.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 9 · 2:11–2:22 · Required tools and languages
<!-- NARRATION -->
The web app uses JavaScript, React, Vite and Web Audio. The native iPhone app uses Swift and SwiftUI, with HealthKit and CoreBluetooth. Seventeen automated tests cover storage, CSV and visit-brief behavior.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 10 · 2:22–2:34 · Coding challenge and solution
<!-- NARRATION -->
A difficult coding problem was stale Bluetooth callbacks. A generation counter rejects readings from old connections. Each accepted reading is labeled live or simulated at the source, and that label stays in exports.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 11 · 2:34–2:43 · Next steps, not invented traction
<!-- NARRATION -->
Next are co-design sessions with intended users and caregivers, followed by usability testing and an iPhone beta. Sensor history in visit briefs is planned, not presented as finished.
<!-- /NARRATION -->

## 12 · 2:43–2:51 · Learning
<!-- NARRATION -->
This project taught me to build for choice, keep demonstrations honest, and distinguish research-informed design from evidence of real-world effectiveness.
<!-- /NARRATION -->
