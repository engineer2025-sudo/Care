# Activity and accessibility design notes

CareSphere's activities are optional, self-paced experiences. They are not validated therapy, treatment, screening, or cognitive assessment. The app should not infer a diagnosis, skill level, or health change from participation.

## Interface choices

- The web app offers a warm-light or charcoal/sage palette, plus separate high-contrast and reduced-motion settings. The native app offers follow-device, light, or dark appearance choices, follows Dynamic Type and system accessibility settings, and can add stronger card/status outlines or slower activity motion. No single color scheme is assumed to suit every autistic person or every person with dementia.
- Keep readable text contrast even when using a restrained palette. Use an icon together with a visible text label; never rely on color alone to communicate a choice or state.
- Keep navigation predictable, controls large, language direct, and feedback calm. Avoid automatic advancement, countdowns, flashing, confetti, or sound that starts without an explicit choice. Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Offer a clear way to pause, repeat, skip, or leave an activity. Keep picture cards visible until the player chooses to hide them.
- Feelings Explorer has no correct answer, streak, or score. People may interpret expressions and describe feelings differently.
- Picture Pairs and Picture Story are untimed and unscored. The stories are examples, not prescriptive daily routines; personal preferences and routines vary.

## Evidence and limits

Autism-oriented serious-game research reports promising outcomes in some domains, but the interventions and study methods vary. A 2025 meta-analysis reported improvements for several tested outcomes, while rating most included trials as having some concerns or high risk of bias. These findings do not establish that these particular CareSphere activities are effective. Research on game-based activities for older adults with cognitive impairment also reports substantial heterogeneity and limitations in evidence quality. Activities here are therefore presented only as optional practice and enjoyment, not as care or treatment.

Design should be reviewed with autistic people, people living with dementia, caregivers, and accessibility specialists before making stronger claims or using the activities in a clinical setting.

## References

- Nicolaidis et al. (2019), *Development of the AASPIRE Web Accessibility Guidelines for Autistic Web Users*. The co-designed guidance recommends simple, consistent navigation and multiple palette/contrast choices because color preferences and vision needs differ. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6485264/
- Alahmari et al. (2023), *Features and effects of computer-based games on cognitive impairments in children with autism spectrum disorder: an evidence-based systematic literature review*. The review includes varied interventions and study designs; it is not evidence for CareSphere's specific activities. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9809031/
- Liu et al. (2025), *Efficacy of gamified digital health interventions for children and adolescents with autism spectrum disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis*. Pooled findings are promising for some tested outcomes, but trial bias and intervention differences limit generalization to CareSphere. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12781649/
- 2024 systematic review of serious games for social skills in autistic children and adolescents. The review describes promising findings while noting a small evidence base and need for more rigorous studies. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38470619/
- 2022 systematic review/meta-analysis of serious games for older adults with cognitive impairment. The authors report possible benefits but very low/heterogeneous evidence quality. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8949701/
- AbilityNet, *Designing for dementia*. Practical guidance emphasizes clear language, visible labels, contrast, orientation, and avoiding unnecessary distraction. https://abilitynet.org.uk/factsheets/designing-dementia
