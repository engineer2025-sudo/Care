# Research and how CareSphere works

This page summarizes the two open-access reviews shared for the app. It separates the studies' findings from CareSphere's design decisions; neither paper evaluated CareSphere.

## Serious games and social skills in autism

Carneiro, T., Carvalho, A., Frota, S., & Filipe, M. G. (2024). *Serious Games for Developing Social Skills in Children and Adolescents with Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Systematic Review*. **Healthcare, 12**(5), 508. [doi:10.3390/healthcare12050508](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare12050508).

The review included nine studies of game interventions for autistic children and adolescents. The included studies reported positive results in at least one measured social-skills-related outcome; the outcomes varied and included emotion recognition or regulation, joint attention, and behavior. The authors also described substantial risk of bias, heterogeneous programs and methods, and a limited evidence base, and called for more rigorous studies and tailored approaches. The study population was children and adolescents; it cannot establish benefit for CareSphere or for older adults.

**CareSphere design response:** the optional *Social moment cards* offer brief, fictional everyday situations and several plausible choices. It does not grade an answer, require eye contact or expression imitation, record the choice, or claim to train or treat social skills. A person can discuss another response or skip. This is a design choice informed by the review's attention to choice and individual fit, not an intervention proven by that review.

## Computerized cognitive interventions for MCI and dementia

Irazoki, E., Contreras-Somoza, L. M., Toribio-Guzmán, J. M., Jenaro-Río, C., van der Roest, H., & Franco-Martín, M. A. (2020). *Technologies for Cognitive Training and Cognitive Rehabilitation for People With Mild Cognitive Impairment and Dementia. A Systematic Review*. **Frontiers in Psychology, 11**, 648. [doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00648](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00648).

The authors selected 19 studies and identified 11 programs. Their review distinguishes cognitive stimulation, cognitive training, and cognitive rehabilitation, and describes differences in intended users, tasks, delivery, personalization, and evidence. It concludes that the programs are not interchangeable and calls for more information and clinical studies to enable comparisons. The review's inclusion criteria and findings do not validate CareSphere's optional standalone activities. In particular, clinician-led individualized rehabilitation is not the same intervention as a self-guided app card.

**CareSphere design response:** activities run locally, are optional and untimed, and include visible steps, hints, skip/stop controls, and the choice to revisit an example. Answers are not scored, collected, or used to adapt a health profile. These are accessibility and autonomy decisions, not proof of cognitive benefit.

## How the app works

- Activity content works offline on web, iPhone, and Android. A session's selected answer is held only in the current screen state and is not saved or sent to a server.
- Favorites are saved locally on the device. They identify a preference, not performance.
- Each activity can be skipped or left. There are no streaks, time limits, or health scores.
- The activities do not diagnose, prevent, slow, or treat dementia or autism, and are not a cognitive or social-skills assessment. They are not a substitute for professional care or a structured, evidence-based program.
- Other features are separate: health, Bluetooth, notifications, and emergency messaging are only available through their own user choices and system permissions. The research page does not imply that every app feature is covered by these activity privacy details.
